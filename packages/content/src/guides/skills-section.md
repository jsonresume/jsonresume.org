---
title: How to write a skills section
description: Group your skills so a reader and an applicant tracking system can find them, and choose the ones that matter for the job.
---

A skills section helps two readers: the recruiter skimming for a match, and the applicant tracking system (ATS) searching for keywords. Both want the same thing: the skills the job asks for, named the way the job names them.

## Group them

A long comma-separated list is hard to read. Group skills by kind, a line each:

- **Languages:** TypeScript, Python, SQL
- **Frontend:** React, Next.js, CSS
- **Cloud:** AWS (Lambda, DynamoDB), Terraform
- **Leadership:** hiring, mentoring, roadmap planning

In JSON Resume each group is a skill with a `name` and its `keywords`, and every theme shows them that way.

## Use the job's words

If the post says "Kubernetes", write "Kubernetes", not just "container orchestration". Spell out abbreviations once if both are common: "Search engine optimisation (SEO)".

## Only what you'd talk about

Every skill you list is fair game in an interview. Leave out tools you used once years ago. "Microsoft Word" and "email" don't need listing for most jobs.

## Show them, too

Skills in a list are claims; skills in your work experience are evidence. The strongest resumes mention the important ones in both places: "Moved billing to Stripe and cut failed payments by a third" proves more than "Stripe" in a list.

## Levels

Self-rated levels ("Expert", five stars) mean little to readers. If you include them, keep them honest and consistent, or leave them out.
