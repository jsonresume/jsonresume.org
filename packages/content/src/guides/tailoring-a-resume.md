---
title: Tailoring your resume to a job
description: Ten minutes per application that make the difference: match the job's words, reorder your strongest evidence, and keep your master resume intact.
---

A resume written for everyone persuades no one in particular. Tailoring doesn't mean rewriting; it means making the evidence that matters for _this_ job the first thing a reader sees.

## In ten minutes

1. **Read the post twice.** Mark the skills and results it asks for, and the words it uses for them.
2. **Change the summary's last line** to say you want this kind of role.
3. **Reorder bullets** so the most relevant result comes first in each job.
4. **Use their words** where they're true: if they say "stakeholder management", don't make the reader translate "worked with other teams".
5. **Move a relevant project up** if it proves something the job needs.
6. **Cut what's irrelevant** to make room.

## Never stretch the truth

Tailoring is choosing and wording what's true. Interviews test every line.

## Keep your master resume

On jsonresume.org, open any job on the [job board](/jobs) and choose _Make a version for this job_: you get a copy of your resume with its own link to tailor, and your main resume doesn't change. An AI assistant connected through [MCP](/mcp) can draft the tailoring for you to review, as a suggestion you accept or reject.
