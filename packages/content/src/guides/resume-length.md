---
title: How long should a resume be?
description: One page or two: a rule of thumb by career stage, what to cut first, and when a longer CV is expected.
---

There's no law, but there is a reader: someone with many applications and a few minutes. Length is a cost you ask them to pay, so every line should earn its place.

## A rule of thumb

- **Students and early career (under about five years):** one page.
- **Mid and senior career:** up to two pages.
- **Academic CVs, and some countries' public sector and medical roles:** longer, with publications, grants and teaching listed in full. Follow the field's convention.

## What to cut first

1. Jobs more than ten to fifteen years ago: keep the title, company and dates.
2. Bullets that describe duties rather than results.
3. Skills nobody would ask you about.
4. "References available on request": it goes without saying.
5. Repeated information: if the summary says it, the bullets don't need to.

## Make it fit, not shrink

Tiny fonts and narrow margins make a resume harder to read, not shorter. Cut words instead. If a resume runs a few lines over a page, the PDF download on jsonresume.org has a fit-to-one-page option (`?fit=1`) that scales it down a little, never more than it must.

## Different lengths for different jobs

Keep a complete master resume, and a shorter [version](/dashboard/resume#variants) for each kind of role, so cutting for one application doesn't lose what you need for the next.
