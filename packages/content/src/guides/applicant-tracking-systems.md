---
title: Resumes and applicant tracking systems
description: What an applicant tracking system does with your resume, what trips it up, and how to make sure it reads yours correctly.
---

Most companies that hire regularly use an applicant tracking system (ATS) such as Greenhouse, Lever or Workday. It stores applications, lets recruiters search them, and often reads your resume into a profile. It rarely "rejects" you on its own, but if it reads your resume badly, a recruiter searching for your skills may never see you.

## What helps an ATS read you

- **Real text, not pictures of text.** Export a PDF from a document or a web page, not a scan.
- **Standard section names:** Experience, Education, Skills, Projects.
- **One reading order.** Simple layouts read cleanly; text in shapes, text boxes or tables can come out scrambled.
- **Dates in a consistent format**, next to each job.
- **Contact details in the body**, not only in a page header or footer.
- **The job's own words** for skills and titles, where they're true for you.

## What trips it up

- Icons standing in for words (a phone icon instead of "Phone").
- Unusual section headings ("Where I've been").
- Several columns that read in the wrong order.
- Important text only in images or graphics.
