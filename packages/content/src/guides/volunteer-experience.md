---
title: Volunteer experience on a resume
description: When volunteering belongs on your resume, where to put it, and how to describe it with the same results you would give a paid job.
---

Volunteering shows skills, commitment and what you care about. It counts most when you are early in your career, changing fields or returning after a break, and when it is relevant to the job.

## Where to put it

- **Its own section**, "Volunteering", after your work experience. This is the usual place.
- **In your work history**, if it was substantial and close to the job you want: a volunteer treasurer applying for finance roles, say. Mark it clearly as a volunteer role.

## Describe it like a job

Organisation, your role, dates, and two or three results:

- "Treasurer, Riverside Food Bank, 2021–present. Keep the books for a £60,000 yearly budget; moved accounts to Xero and cut month-end from a week to a day."
- "Volunteer coach, under-12 football, 2022–2023. Coached 15 players weekly; the team finished second in the league."

## What to leave out

One-off events and causes unrelated to the job can go, unless your resume is short and they show something useful. Political and religious work is your call: include it if it shows relevant skills and you are comfortable discussing it.
