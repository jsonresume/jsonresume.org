title: Writing your first resume
description: No work experience yet? What to put in instead: projects, study, volunteering and part-time work, written to show what you can do.
---

Everyone starts with an empty "Experience" section. Employers hiring for a first job know that; they're looking for evidence that you can learn, finish things and work with people. You have more of it than you think.

## What counts

- **Projects:** coursework, personal projects, things you built or organised. Say what it was, what you did and how it turned out.
- **Part-time and casual work:** retail, hospitality, tutoring, babysitting. Reliability and dealing with the public are real skills.
- **Volunteering and clubs:** roles you held, events you ran, money you raised.
- **Study:** your qualification, relevant subjects, a strong grade or award.

## Order it for the reader

Put your strongest section first. For many first resumes that's Education or Projects, then Experience.

## Write results, even small ones

- "Ran the school's coding club: grew it from 6 to 25 members in a year."
- "Served 100+ customers a shift at a busy café; trusted to open and close."
- "Built a budgeting app in React for a class project; 30 classmates used it."

## Keep it to one page

One full page beats two thin ones.

## A summary that points forward

Two lines on what you're studying or good at, and the kind of role you want: "Business student with retail experience, looking for a graduate role in operations."
