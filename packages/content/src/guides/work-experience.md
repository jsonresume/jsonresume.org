---
title: How to write your work experience
description: Turn duties into results: a simple pattern for each bullet point, how many to write, and how to show numbers when you don't have them.
---

Work experience is the heart of a resume, and most people write it as a list of duties. Recruiters already know what a "project manager" does. What they want to know is what happened because _you_ did it.

## One pattern for every bullet

**What you did, how, and what changed.**

- _Duty:_ "Responsible for customer support emails."
- _Result:_ "Answered 60 support emails a day and cut the average reply time from two days to four hours by writing a library of templated answers."

Start each bullet with a verb in the past tense (present tense for your current job): _led, built, cut, grew, shipped, trained, negotiated_.

## Numbers, even rough ones

Numbers make a claim believable: people, money, time, volume, percentages. If you don't know the exact figure, use an honest estimate and say so in the interview: "around 200 customers", "roughly 30% faster". Never invent one.

No numbers at all? Show scope instead: who used it, how often, what it replaced, who asked for it next.

## How many bullets

- **Recent and relevant jobs:** three to six.
- **Older or unrelated jobs:** one or two, or just the title and dates.
- **Ten or more years back:** you can group them under "Earlier roles" with one line each.

## Order matters

Put the bullet most relevant to the job you're applying for first. Readers skim the first one or two and move on.
